# Agents are autonomous except when they are not (which is most of the time)

**Type**: Analysis/Reality Check
**Source**: Technical essay on agent autonomy limitations

## Summary

A critical examination of the gap between "autonomous agent" marketing and operational reality. Most deployed agents require frequent human intervention, approval gates, and course correction. True autonomy is the exception, not the rule, and the framing matters for expectation-setting.

## Key Takeaways

- **"Autonomous" is often marketing** — most production agents run within guardrails requiring human sign-off
- **Intervention points are everywhere** — approval gates, budget caps, unsafe operation detection, context clarity
- **Autonomy exists on a spectrum** — pure autonomy is rare; most agents are semi-autonomous with human steering
- **Human-in-the-loop is the realistic default** — not a failure mode, but the necessary operating model
- **Cost control requires intervention** — without human oversight, agents can easily spiral into expensive, wasteful loops
