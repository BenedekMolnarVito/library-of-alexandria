# Product-Minded Engineers in an AI-Native World

**Authors**: The Pragmatic Engineer (host); Tuomas Artman, Michelle Lim, Drew Hoskins (panelists)
**Date**: 2026-03
**Type**: video-transcript

## Summary

Panel discussion from The Pragmatic Summit featuring Tuomas Artman (co-founder/CTO of Linear), Michelle Lim (co-founder of Flint, ex-founding engineer at Warp), and Drew Hoskins (staff engineer turned staff PM at Stripe and Temporal, author of "The Product-Minded Engineer"). The panel defines a product engineer as someone motivated by user impact who can own the full cycle from problem definition to implementation — contrasted with a code-minded engineer focused on technical complexity.

Discussion covers how to develop the muscle, the role of "taste" as pattern-matching shaped by customer proximity and deep system knowledge, and how AI is reshaping what the role means when AI can handle much of the implementation. Linear hired exclusively product engineers with no PMs until 30+ employees. Michelle's reframing — dropping frontend/backend as axes, using product/infra engineering instead — clarifies the culture Linear and Flint were building. Drew's journey from staff engineer to staff PM illustrates the spectrum is learnable, not innate.

## Key Takeaways

- Product engineer = engineer with PM sprinkled on top; cares equally about "what and why" as "how"
- Linear hired exclusively product engineers and had no PMs until 30+ employees — product engineering as a cultural strategy
- Michelle Lim's insight: "stop using frontend/backend to describe engineering" — the better axis is product engineering vs. code/infra engineering
- Drew Hoskins: staff engineer → staff PM — product-mindedness is a learnable muscle, not binary
- Tuomas: product engineering applies even to infrastructure — rewrote Linear's sync engine 4x for user experience, not technical elegance
- "Taste" is critical — pattern-matching what good looks like, shaped by customer proximity and deep system knowledge
- AI is shifting the engineer role: less time on implementation, more on problem definition and taste/judgment — the product-engineering mindset becomes more valuable, not less
- Flint's autonomous website platform: customer sites now built overnight by AI agents
