# I Ditched Warp. My Free zshrc Does 90% of What It Does.

**Authors**: Alex Dunlop
**Date**: 2026-04
**Type**: article

## Summary

Alex Dunlop argues that Warp terminal's $18/month subscription is unnecessary for experienced developers, and shares the exact .zshrc configuration and six free brew-installable tools that replicate Warp's core features (autocomplete, fuzzy history search, smart prompt, directory jumping). The piece is a reaction to Warp's increasingly 'mobile-game-like' upsell UI and paywall-gated features. This article is tangentially connected to the AI/tech batch — Warp has AI baked into the terminal (AI error explanations, AI command suggestions) — and the author's preference for composable, minimal open-source tools mirrors the philosophy of the agent framework comparison and local-AI movement more broadly.

## Key Takeaways

- Six free tools replace Warp: zsh-autosuggestions, zsh-syntax-highlighting, fzf (Ctrl+R fuzzy history), Starship (prompt UI), zoxide (smart cd), eza (ls replacement), bat (cat replacement)
- Total setup time: 20 minutes
- Warp is still valid for beginners or teams that need shared workflows/prompts
- The author's minimal .zshrc is shared verbatim — only uses a subset of the 6 tools
- Terminal should be 'fast, quiet, and free'
