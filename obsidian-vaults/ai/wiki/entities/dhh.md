# DHH (David Heinemeier Hansson)

David Heinemeier Hansson — universally known as DHH — is the creator of Ruby on Rails, co-founder of [[wiki/entities/37signals]] (the company behind Basecamp and Hey), and one of the most prominent voices in software engineering who engages seriously with AI coding tools while resisting the uncritical maximalism typical of AI boosterism.

## Background / History

DHH created Ruby on Rails in 2004 while building Basecamp, and the framework's emphasis on developer happiness, convention over configuration, and integrated full-stack development shaped an entire generation of web developers. He has been a consistent advocate for developer craft, small teams over large ones, and products that serve their users rather than their investors. His public persona combines technical authority with a contrarian streak — he has been as willing to criticize open-source culture, VC funding models, and remote work orthodoxy as he has been to build against the grain.

## Key Contributions / Features

**Agent-First Development Stance**: DHH has publicly embraced AI coding assistants — including Claude Code and similar tools — as genuine productivity multipliers, while refusing to endorse "vibe coding" (writing code by prompting without understanding what's produced). His position is that AI tools are powerful precisely when wielded by engineers who understand what good code looks like, and that using AI as a replacement for judgment rather than an amplifier of it is a mistake. (Source: [[wiki/sources/dhh-new-way-writing-code-agent-first]])

**Craft-Preserving Adoption Model**: DHH's framework for AI adoption at [[wiki/entities/37signals]] emphasizes using AI as a tool that accelerates skilled engineers rather than replacing them — maintaining code quality, ownership, and understanding while capturing the speed benefits. This is a counterpoint to both the "AI will replace programmers" narrative and the "AI is just autocomplete" dismissal.

**Public Skepticism of Vibe Coding**: DHH has been pointed in his criticism of the tendency to use AI to generate code that the developer doesn't understand, maintain, or take responsibility for — connecting to the "dark code" concerns raised by [[wiki/entities/nate-b-jones]].

## Role in AI Landscape

DHH represents a pragmatist-craftsman perspective in the AI coding debate — neither a luddite nor an uncritical adopter. His voice carries weight because he has a demonstrated history of building successful software products with small teams and strong opinions about what makes code good. His engagement with agent-first development signals that even the most craft-focused engineering culture is finding genuine value in AI tools, which is arguably more meaningful than enthusiasm from communities that were never skeptical.
