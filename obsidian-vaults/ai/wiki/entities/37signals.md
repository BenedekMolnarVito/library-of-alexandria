# 37signals

37signals is the software company behind Basecamp (project management) and Hey (email), founded by [[wiki/entities/dhh]] (David Heinemeier Hansson) and Jason Fried. It is known for a distinctive engineering culture that emphasizes small teams, long-term thinking, and craft over growth-at-all-costs — making its engagement with AI coding tools a culturally significant signal about where pragmatic, quality-focused engineering organizations are landing on AI adoption.

## Background / History

37signals was founded in 1999 as a web design consultancy and pivoted to building Basecamp (originally Backpack) in the early 2000s. The company is self-funded, has never taken VC investment, and has operated profitably for decades with a small team (historically around 50–60 employees). DHH's creation of Ruby on Rails came directly from building Basecamp, and the company has consistently used its own products as a laboratory for its engineering philosophy. The team's writings — including the books *Getting Real*, *Rework*, and *It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work* — have shaped a generation of independent software companies.

## Key Contributions / Features

**Pragmatist AI Adoption Model**: 37signals' approach to AI coding tools, as articulated by [[wiki/entities/dhh]], represents a "craft-preserving adoption" model — using AI to amplify skilled engineers rather than to replace engineering judgment. This is a carefully articulated middle position between AI maximalism and resistance, and its adoption by a company known for strong engineering opinions gives it credibility as a template for quality-focused teams. (Source: [[wiki/sources/dhh-new-way-writing-code-agent-first]])

**Skepticism of Vibe Coding**: DHH and by extension 37signals has been public about the risks of vibe coding — using AI to generate code that developers don't understand or maintain — connecting to concerns about code quality, organizational knowledge, and technical debt in the AI era.

**Small Team + AI Amplification**: 37signals' existing emphasis on small, highly capable teams makes it a natural fit for AI-augmented development — AI tools effectively act as leverage multipliers that allow the company's small headcount to maintain throughput as products grow in complexity.

## Role in AI Landscape

37signals' AI engagement story is significant precisely because the company has historically been skeptical of technology fashions and focused on fundamentals. If even 37signals — with its explicit commitment to craft and sustainability over hype — is actively integrating AI coding tools, it signals that AI-assisted development has passed the threshold from speculative toy to practical tool. The company's articulation of principled AI adoption (tools should amplify judgment, not replace it) provides a useful framework for other engineering-culture-first organizations navigating the same question.
